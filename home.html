Overview

Canada offers world-class cold-water diving, featuring pristine shipwrecks in Ontario's Great Lakes, vibrant marine life in British Columbia, and unique iceberg dives in Newfoundland. Top spots include Tobermory's wrecks, Port Hardy's, BC, nutrient-rich currents, and the Thousand Islands' sunken history. Drysuits are generally recommended.

Top Featured Destinations

Tobermory, Ontario: Known as the "Shipwreck Capital of the Great Lakes," Tobermory offers over 20 accessible wrecks, including the famous SS City of Milwaukee and the Sweepstakes. Divers can explore these well-preserved sites in clear waters, making it a top destination for wreck diving enthusiasts.
Port Hardy, British Columbia: Located on Vancouver Island, Port Hardy is renowned for its nutrient-rich waters that attract a diverse array of marine life. Divers can encounter giant Pacific octopuses, wolf eels, and vibrant rockfish while exploring kelp forests and underwater caves.
Newfoundland: Experience unique iceberg dives and encounter marine wildlife in the cold waters of Newfoundland.The unique diving experience offers a chance to witness the dynamic relationship between ice and ocean, with opportunities to see marine life that thrives in these cold waters, making it an unforgettable adventure for divers seeking something truly extraordinary.
Thousand Islands, Ontario: This region boasts a variety of shipwrecks scattered throughout the St. Lawrence River. Notable dives include the wreck of the General Brock and the Aloha, offering divers a chance to explore history beneath the waves while enjoying abundant freshwater marine life.
